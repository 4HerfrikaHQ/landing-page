# Build Academy courses on our own stack, with lesson content in Prismic

> Amended by [ADR 0003](./0003-completion-policy-per-course.md): completion rules (pass mark, attempts, watch tracking, Tasks) are now set per Course through a Completion policy, and the rules below are the default policy. Partner videos move from unlisted YouTube to private storage with signed URLs, and Learners see a revoked Certificate as "withdrawn". Dates below are as of 11 Nov 2026.

We are building a place on 4herfrika.org where anyone can sign up, take a free AI-literacy course made with Ordina, track their progress, pass short quizzes and earn a certificate that carries both logos and that an employer can check with one link. We will build it inside the website we already run (Next.js on Vercel, Supabase, Prismic) instead of adopting a separate learning platform. Lesson content (text, slides, video links, quiz questions) is written by 4HerFrika admins in Prismic, the editor they already use for the blog and the homepage. Everything about a learner (account, progress, quiz scores, certificates) lives in our Supabase database. For launch we build for one partner and a handful of courses, but partners are their own content type from day one, so the next partner is new content, not a rebuild. The conference is on **27 November 2026**, 16 days (12 working days) after this record, so we launch in two steps: sign-up and the first module at the conference, then certificates and the rest of the course in December. Status stays **proposed** until Ordina confirms the content dates and the completion rules (see open questions).

## Decision

- **Launch in two steps.** *Conference, Fri 27 Nov:* email-code sign-up, course page, lesson player, quizzes and saved progress, with Module 1 published. *By Fri 11 Dec:* the remaining modules, then certificates, the check page, LinkedIn sharing, the share image, Google sign-in and "My learning". *By Fri 18 Dec:* the admin stats page, CSV export, print layout and the "certificate ready" email. Until the stats page exists, pilot numbers come from SQL on the same tables. Certificates switch on only once every module is published, because course completion counts published lessons, so a learner can't "finish" a one-module course at the conference.
- **What's in the MVP (both steps).** A course catalogue, a course page, sign-up and sign-in, a lesson player for video, slides and text, quizzes, progress saved per lesson, a "My learning" page, certificates with a public verification page and LinkedIn sharing, Prismic authoring for admins, and an admin stats page. Site chrome in en/fr/sw, as today.
- **What's out for launch.** Partner dashboards and partner self-service authoring, payments, cohort-based intakes and application gates, discussion or comments, live sessions, assignments and peer review, offline downloads or an app, Open Badges files, server-generated PDFs, course content in more than one language (unless Ordina supplies translations), gamification, SCORM import, links to the mentorship side.
- **Build, don't adopt.** The course system is a set of pages and five tables inside this repo, at `app/[locale]/(website)/academy/`, following the route folder convention (`_actions`, `_schema`, `_components` colocated). It reuses Supabase Auth, Drizzle, next-safe-action, Resend, the shadcn/Base UI components and the existing `/dashboard/admin` shell.
- **Content model.** Three Prismic types: `partner` (name, logo, website), `course` (title, summary, cover, partners with a role, completion settings, and its modules as a slice zone where each `module` slice is a title plus repeatable links to lessons, because Prismic groups can't nest), and `lesson` (kind: `video`, `slides`, `text` or `quiz`, with the body as slices). Quiz questions are a slice on a `quiz` lesson (question, 2–4 options, the correct option, an optional explanation). Learner data in Postgres refers to Prismic **document IDs**, never UIDs, so renaming a course's URL doesn't orphan anyone's progress.
- **Authoring.** 4HerFrika admins enter content in Prismic from material Ordina sends. The Prismic API is switched to **private** and the access token stays server-side, because quiz answers live in Prismic and the API is public today (`prismicio.ts` creates the client without a token). The quiz page never sends correct answers to the browser. Grading happens in a server action.
- **Video.** Unlisted YouTube videos, shown through the `YouTubeEmbed` component in `@next/third-parties`. We already ship that package for GA4, and it wraps Paul Irish's `lite-youtube-embed`, so the page shows a thumbnail and loads the player only when tapped. No new dependency. Every video lesson shows its length and has a text version underneath. Slides are exported as images, uploaded to Prismic and served one at a time through Prismic's image CDN (WebP, sized to the screen). No video files on Supabase or Vercel.
- **Accounts.** Supabase Auth, the same service the staff dashboard uses, with two ways in: a 6-digit email code (as in `app/(dashboard)/dashboard/login/_actions.ts`, but with `shouldCreateUser: true`) and "Continue with Google" (the standard redirect flow, not Google One Tap). Learners get their own `learners` table (name as it should appear on certificates, country, optional university/campus), separate from `users`, which stays staff and mentors only. One login can be both a mentor and a learner. Cloudflare Turnstile is switched on through Supabase's built-in captcha support. That setting is project-wide, so the staff login form needs the Turnstile widget too. The "Confirm signup" and "Magic link" email templates both change to show the 6-digit code (`{{ .Token }}`), because new learners get the first template and returning ones the second. `proxy.ts` refreshes the Supabase session on Academy routes as well as `/dashboard`. Those routes are locale-prefixed (`/fr/academy/...`), so the refresh has to run alongside next-intl routing and pass on its response, not replace it.
- **Progress and completion.** A lesson is complete when the learner reaches its end and taps "Continue" (text, slides, video) or passes it (quiz). A course is complete when every **required** lesson is complete and every quiz is passed at its pass mark. Per course, admins set the pass mark (default 70%) and can mark lessons optional. Quizzes can be retaken, at most 3 attempts per quiz per hour, with options shuffled. A failed attempt shows the score, not which answers were right. Each quiz has at least 10 questions, so passing 70% by guessing has a chance of roughly 1 in 300 per attempt.
- **Certificates.** Issued automatically, in the same database transaction that records the final completion. Each gets a short random code and a public page at `4herfrika.org/certificates/<code>` showing the learner's name, course, date, both logos and a valid or revoked status. The page has a share image made with `next/og` (as the Career Corner share images are), an "Add to LinkedIn profile" button (LinkedIn allows one issuing organisation, so 4HerFrika is the issuer and Ordina is named in the certificate title), and a print stylesheet for saving as PDF. The certificate stores a snapshot of the name, course title and partner logos at issue time, so later content edits don't change it. The email that says "your certificate is ready" goes out after the transaction commits.
- **Open Badges: not yet.** The certificate record keeps the fields Open Badges 3.0 needs (issuer, recipient, achievement, criteria, issue date, unique ID), so the same certificates can be published as badges later without reissuing.
- **Analytics.** The numbers come from our own tables, shown on a new `/dashboard/admin/academy` page with charts drawn the way `mentor/stats` does it (recharts): sign-ups, enrolments, how far people get, completions, quiz pass rates and per-question results, and certificates issued, with a CSV export for Ordina. Browser-side steps (course viewed, sign-up started, certificate shared) go to the GA4 we already run, as new events in `src/lib/analytics.ts`. That helper only runs in the browser, so quiz and certificate counts come from the database, not GA4. No new analytics tool.
- **Launch audience.** People on the Academy waitlist (`academy_waitlist_entries`) asked to hear when the Academy opens. They get one launch email through Resend, with an unsubscribe link.

## Considered options

- **Hosted LMS (Thinkific, Teachable, LearnWorlds, TalentLMS):** fastest to a working course, but learners leave our site for a vendor domain and account, certificate and analytics design are the vendor's, and the bill is monthly with tier caps on courses, admins or learners. Setting one up for the conference alone would take the same two weeks of content entry, then a migration in December. If step one slips, the fallback is to open sign-ups at the conference and the course on 1 December, not a second platform.
- **Self-hosted open-source LMS (Moodle, Open edX, Canvas):** free licences, but each is a separate server, login, look and upgrade cycle for a volunteer team to run, and much heavier on a phone than our own pages. Open edX is aimed at universities with ops staff.
- **Newer open-source LMSs (LearnHouse, ClassroomIO, Frappe LMS, CourseLit):** each ships courses, quizzes or assignments, and certificates out of the box. LearnHouse is Next.js + FastAPI + Postgres + Redis, ClassroomIO is SvelteKit + Hono + Postgres + Redis, Frappe LMS is Python/Vue, and CourseLit is Next.js + MongoDB. All are AGPL-3.0, so changes we serve must be published. Each is a second app with its own server, login and look, and some features (SSO, payments) sit behind an enterprise licence. Caveat: LearnHouse or ClassroomIO on a small VM at `learn.4herfrika.org` is the fastest route to a working platform with certificates, which makes it the fallback if step one can't land in 12 days. The cost is a December migration of accounts and progress.
- **Course Builder (Skill Recordings, behind Total TypeScript and Epic Web):** no longer public. Its GitHub repo returns 404, and only `course-builder-local` and `coursebuilder-cli` remain, so there's nothing to adopt.
- **Hybrid (our site for the catalogue, an LMS for lessons via LTI or embeds):** two logins or an SSO integration, and progress lives in someone else's database. It costs most of the build and most of the LMS bill.
- **Quiz questions in Postgres, entered through an admin form:** keeps answers out of the CMS entirely, but means building a quiz editor before launch. We keep this in reserve in case making the Prismic API private causes trouble.
- **Content as MDX files in the repo:** a common pattern for developer-run courses (The Odin Project keeps its whole curriculum as files in a public repo). Each lesson is one MDX file (`content/course/<module>/<lesson>.mdx`) with `title`, `video`, `duration` and `resources` in the frontmatter. A tool like Velite turns the files into typed data, and a `comingSoon` flag releases lessons gradually. We already have `@next/mdx` for `content/privacy`, so this skips the Prismic types, private API and token, and would save 2–3 of the 12 days. Caveats: this repo is **public**, so quiz answer keys can't live in the MDX and would go in a database table instead, entered with `bun db:studio`. Every content edit needs an engineer and a deploy. We keep Prismic as the decision and switch course 1 to MDX only if step-one work is behind on Fri 13 Nov (open question 12).
- **Video on Vimeo:** private-link videos played through `react-player`. There are no ads or suggested videos, embeds can be restricted to our domain, and quality adapts to the connection. It needs a paid plan, so confirm the price. This is the first fallback if Ordina doesn't want YouTube.
- **Video on Mux via `next-video`, or Cloudflare Stream or Bunny Stream:** no ads, signed playback, our own player. Delivery is billed by the minute or GB, which grows with every learner. `next-video` (MIT, from Mux) also supports Vercel Blob, S3 and R2, but its own comparison shows only Mux adapts quality to slow networks. Mux's `video-course-starter-kit` is a reference for upload and playback, though its stack (PlanetScale, GitHub login) is dated.
- **Video files on Supabase Storage or Vercel Blob:** no adaptive quality, so a learner on a weak connection downloads the full-size file. Ruled out for data cost.
- **Phone number (SMS or WhatsApp) sign-in:** suits phone-first users, but every code costs money and the providers need setup. Revisit if many sign-ups abandon at the email step.
- **Passwords:** more support (resets) and weaker security than codes, for no gain.
- **Watch-time tracking on videos:** easy to fake and adds the YouTube player API. Quizzes carry certificate integrity instead.
- **Open Badges 3.0 from day one:** standard, machine-verifiable credentials, but they need signing keys, JSON-LD and an issuer profile. LinkedIn doesn't read them, and employers will click the link anyway.
- **Credential services (Credly, Accredible, Certifier):** polished badges and wallets, priced per credential or by annual contract, and verification lives on their domain.
- **Precedent for our own check page:** freeCodeCamp, the largest open-source learning site, uses the same model. Each learner's certificate has a public page (`freecodecamp.org/certification/<user>/<cert>`) and an "Add to LinkedIn" button.
- **MIT Digital Credentials Consortium:** open-source Open Badges 3.0 issuer, Learner Credential Wallet and VerifierPlus. This is the toolkit when we add Open Badges. Caveat: its `issuer-coordinator` app hasn't been updated since November 2024.
- **A product analytics tool (PostHog, Amplitude):** better funnels, but another tool and another processor in the privacy policy. Our tables already answer the pilot's questions.

## Consequences

- **Learner journey to design:** landing on `/academy` → course page → sign up (email code or Google) → name for certificate → lesson player → quiz → course complete → certificate page → share to LinkedIn → an employer opens the link and sees "Valid".
- **Admin journey:** receive material from Ordina → create or reuse the `partner` → create `lesson` documents → create the `course`, add lessons to modules, set the pass mark → preview → publish (the existing `/api/revalidate` webhook refreshes the site) → watch `/dashboard/admin/academy`.
- **Screens the designer needs:** catalogue (on `/academy`), course page (logged out and logged in, with progress), sign-up and sign-in (code entry, Google, Turnstile), profile (certificate name), lesson player in four variants (video, slides, text, quiz), quiz result (pass and fail), course complete, "My learning", certificate page (owner view and public view, valid and revoked), certificate share image, certificate print layout, "Certificate ready" email, admin stats page. Mobile first, at 360px wide. Every video has captions, and its text version doubles as the transcript.
- **Staff login edge:** a learner who tries `/dashboard/login` signs in successfully, then hits "unauthorized". That screen needs a line pointing learners to the Academy.
- **Five new tables:** `learners`, `enrolments`, `lesson_completions`, `quiz_attempts`, `certificates`. They follow the repo's text-status-plus-Zod pattern and run through the existing Drizzle migrations at deploy.
- **Prismic becomes part of course logic, not just marketing.** A deleted or unpublished lesson changes what "complete" means for an in-progress course. Admins archive lessons instead of deleting them once learners have started.
- **The privacy policy (`content/privacy`, en/fr/sw) must cover learner accounts, Google sign-in, Cloudflare Turnstile, YouTube embeds and public certificate pages** before launch. Deleting an account deletes the learner's progress, and their certificate pages then show "no longer available". Certificate pages are `noindex` so learners' names don't show up in search.
- **Risk: content arrives late.** This is the critical path, not the code. Mitigation: Module 1 (videos, slides, text, quiz) must be in Prismic by Wed 18 Nov, and the full course by Fri 4 Dec. If Module 1 misses its date, the conference opens sign-ups with a "course opens 1 December" page.
- **Risk: 12 working days for step one.** Mitigation: step one is only sign-in, the course page, the player, quizzes and progress. Designs for those screens are needed by Mon 16 Nov, and engineering starts on the data and sign-in before the designs land. Anything not ready by Wed 25 Nov moves to December, not into a late night.
- **Risk: a sign-up spike at the conference hits email limits.** Nothing in the repo shows production Supabase uses custom SMTP (`[auth.email.smtp]` is commented out in `supabase/config.toml`). Supabase's built-in sender allows only a handful of emails an hour. Mitigation: point Supabase at Resend's SMTP, raise the Supabase email rate limit, check the Resend plan has headroom, and offer Google sign-in, which sends no email. Rehearse with 50 sign-ups.
- **Risk: venue Wi-Fi and phone data.** Mitigation: small server-rendered pages, video behind a tap, text versions, and a "start at home" QR code instead of asking people to watch at the event.
- **Risk: quiz answers leak.** Mitigation: private Prismic API, server-side grading, no answer feedback on failed attempts, attempt limits. A determined person can still guess their way through over a day of retries, and shared answer sheets are accepted for launch. The certificate says the course was completed, not that it was a proctored exam. Question pools are the later fix.
- **Risk: spam accounts.** Mitigation: Turnstile, Supabase rate limits, and email or Google verification before any progress is saved. Spam accounts can't earn certificates without passing quizzes.
- **Risk: YouTube ads or suggested videos look unprofessional next to Ordina's brand.** Mitigation: thumbnail-first embeds now, Vimeo as the swap, and Mux after that.
- **Risk: scope creep toward multi-partner features.** Mitigation: the out-of-scope list above. Partner dashboards get their own ADR when a second partner signs.
- **Cost and time:** roughly 5–6 developer-weeks in total, with step one about two of them, and about $0–20 a month extra (mostly Resend email volume). Everything else runs on services we already pay for.
- **We own the maintenance.** A small team keeps the course system running. It's the same stack and the same people as the rest of the site, so there is nothing new to learn or patch.

## Open questions (each with a recommendation)

1. **Content dates:** can Ordina deliver Module 1 by Wed 18 Nov and the full course by Fri 4 Dec? Recommendation: yes, or we launch sign-ups only at the conference (the conference itself is fixed for Fri 27 Nov).
2. **Courses and lessons at launch:** recommendation: one course, 4–6 modules, under 2 hours in total, videos under 6 minutes each.
3. **Expected learners:** recommendation: plan for 2,000 accounts in the first month and 300 sign-ups in the conference hour. It decides email limits, not hosting.
4. **Exact completion rule:** recommendation: all required lessons, plus every quiz at 70% or higher, with retakes allowed at up to 3 an hour.
5. **Certificate design and signatories:** recommendation: one signatory from each organisation (the 4HerFrika founder and an Ordina lead), with signature images supplied as transparent PNGs, and a landscape layout the designer owns.
6. **Course language:** recommendation: English content at launch, with the site around it in en/fr/sw. Ordina decides whether to fund French and Swahili versions.
7. **Can Ordina's videos go on YouTube (unlisted, on a 4HerFrika channel)?** Recommendation: yes. If Ordina says no, Vimeo on a paid plan with domain-restricted embeds.
8. **What learner data does Ordina receive?** Recommendation: aggregates and the CSV without emails, unless learners opt in to being contacted by Ordina.
9. **Which Academy does this sit under?** The site already has Tech, Business and Climate waitlists. Recommendation: Tech Academy, with the course catalogue open to all three later.
10. **Pilot success:** what numbers make the pilot a success? Recommendation: 40% of sign-ups start a course and 25% of starters earn a certificate within 60 days.
11. **Minimum age:** recommendation: 16+, confirmed by a checkbox at sign-up, matching the university audience.
12. **Prismic or MDX for course 1?** Recommendation: Prismic. If the private API, types and sign-in aren't done by Fri 13 Nov, switch course 1 to MDX in the repo, with quiz answers in the database, and move it to Prismic in December.
